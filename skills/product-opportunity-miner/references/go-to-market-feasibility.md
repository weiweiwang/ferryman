# Go-To-Market Feasibility

Use this reference after demand signals are collected and before final scoring. The goal is to avoid attractive ideas that cannot acquire users or survive competition.

## Existing Solutions

For each serious candidate, check:

- Direct competitors: tools that claim to solve the same job.
- Substitutes: spreadsheets, templates, Notion, ChatGPT, calculators, agencies, manual services, forum advice, or generic platform features.
- Incumbent strength: brand trust, SEO authority, app ratings, pricing, integrations, distribution, and breadth.
- Gap: what users still complain about after knowing these tools exist.
- Wedge: a narrower audience, geography, workflow step, file format, platform, or output that incumbents do not serve well.

Downgrade when existing tools solve the exact job cheaply and clearly, especially when users are not complaining about them.

## SEO Feasibility

When SEO is a primary acquisition path, run a lightweight SERP check for likely queries. Do not invent keyword volume.

Look for:

- Queries checked: record the exact search queries used and evidence URLs from the visible SERP or opened result pages.
- Search intent with product shape: `calculator`, `generator`, `template`, `tracker`, `planner`, `converter`, `checklist`, `spreadsheet`, `app`, `tool`, `how to`, `for <niche>`.
- SERP accessibility: small tools, indie sites, calculators, templates, forum pages, GitHub repos, or weak content ranking alongside large sites.
- Reachable result types: note whether small sites, forums, templates, free tools, app listings, or weak content appear.
- Small-site evidence: record at least one small or non-incumbent result when arguing that SEO is reachable.
- SERP risk: state the main ranking risk, such as official docs, government pages, high-authority publishers, entrenched SaaS, or local intent.
- Long-tail structure: country, profession, platform, file type, role, event, season, or compliance variant that can become many pages.
- Tool-first advantage: free calculator, importer, analyzer, checklist generator, CSV/PDF export, or shareable report that is more useful than an article.
- Community-language match: Reddit/app-review phrases appear naturally in search queries.

Downgrade when:

- Results are dominated by official documentation, government pages, high-authority publishers, or entrenched SaaS with no narrow wedge.
- Queries are purely informational and do not imply a tool, template, or repeated workflow.
- The opportunity needs SEO but has no clear keyword modifier set.

## Acquisition Reality

Prefer opportunities with at least one low-touch channel:

- SEO pages around calculators, templates, checklists, converters, generators, or comparison queries.
- ASO for narrow app-store search terms.
- Community launch posts where the free tool directly answers the thread's pain.
- Directories and template marketplaces.
- Shareable outputs that invite collaborators or repeat visits.
- Programmatic pages from user-selectable dimensions such as country, platform, profession, file type, or task.

Downgrade when acquisition requires cold outbound, demos, partner deals, paid ads, influencer relationships, or trust transfer from mature incumbents.

## Commercial Path

Identify the first plausible paid trigger:

- Paid export: PDF, CSV, calendar, branded report, bulk processing.
- Saved history or reminders.
- More scenarios, templates, or professional presets.
- Team/client sharing.
- One-time purchase for a niche utility.
- Subscription only when repeat value is obvious.
- Affiliate/ads only when purchase intent or traffic scale is realistic.

Downgrade when users would use the tool once, avoid paying, or expect it to be a free commodity.

## Decision Guidance

Use:

- `build_now` when demand, wedge, one-month MVP, competition gap, and acquisition channel are all credible.
- `seo_validate_first` when the product is simple and useful but search accessibility is unproven.
- `validate_page` when demand is plausible but the format, price, or channel is still uncertain.
- `observe` when signals are real but too scattered.
- `reject` when competition, acquisition, or operational risk overwhelms the wedge.
